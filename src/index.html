2026 presentations

01Autonomous unmanned systemsERNA Fire Control System · William Love

William Love, an active-duty U.S. Army Noncommissioned Officer in Charge of Lightning Labs, the innovation lab for the 25th Infantry Division, presented the ERNA Fire Control System: Asymmetric Drone Guidance & Operator Survivability. The project demonstrated a cost-effective retrofit guidance system that uses optical flow navigation to guide unmodified commercial drones in GPS-denied environments. It also physically decouples the human operator from the RF signature.

02AI-augmented open-source developmentGoose in the Machine · Josh Noll

Josh Noll, Data Systems Engineer for 3rd Special Forces Group, U.S. Army, presented Goose in the Machine. The talk traced a first open-source code contribution through a small bug and work with Goose, an open-source general-purpose AI agent developed by Block. It followed the contribution from understanding the problem and locating the relevant function through implementing the fix and securing project approval, and showed how AI can serve as a technical coach rather than a shortcut.

03Embedded systems securityMITRE eCTF pilot retrospective · Matt Lombardo

Matt Lombardo, an embedded systems and security engineer for the U.S. Army, presented a retrospective on Arrowhead Research’s Spring 2025 MITRE Embedded Capture the Flag (eCTF) pilot. The exercise brought together participants from operational, academic, and engineering backgrounds to analyze and work against secure embedded systems in a constrained, time-bounded setting. The work looked at how team composition and workflow influenced vulnerability discovery, and at what it would take to scale the pilot into a repeatable platform for technical talent development.

04Android malware detectionProject Totem · Max Kalichenko

Max Kalichenko, an AI Technician at the U.S. Army Artificial Intelligence Integration Center (AI2C), presented Project Totem. The project is a malware detection tool for Android devices that scans the system and reports a security status. It analyzes applications and device behavior for indicators of malicious activity, with the aim of remaining lightweight enough for everyday use.

05LoRa-based personnel trackingHokie Indago · Virginia Tech Army ROTC

The Virginia Tech Army ROTC team presented Hokie Indago, a personnel-tracking system built from commercial off-the-shelf parts. Wearable LoRa nodes in a Meshtastic mesh stream real-time GNSS position data into a Windows-based PyQt5 interface over open-source maps, for command-and-control accountability in austere cadet training environments. The team was Hunter Lostumo, Benjamin Hardin, Tyler Grogan, Kyle Schmidt, Nate Lagrange, and Luke Moore.

06Contested RF field networkingBlack Relay retrospective · Charles “Jake” Wright and Marcel

Charles “Jake” Wright, an active-duty U.S. Petty Officer First Class serving as a lead military targeter in the Navy’s Undersea Warfare enterprise, presented a retrospective on Black Relay. Black Relay was an Arrowhead Research technical field exercise that followed a development sprint with live red-team testing and controlled operational pacing in a contested RF and communications environment. Field testing took place in and around Southern Pines, North Carolina, from August through November 2025.

07Spectrum exploitation researchVelvet Dynamo · Travis Williams

Travis Williams, an active-duty U.S. Army Master Sergeant and Director of Strategic Partnerships at Arrowhead Research, presented Velvet Dynamo. The project examined Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast (ADS-B) systems used by commercial aircraft for real-time tracking, and the use of the 1090 MHz ADS-B channel as a carrier for other data. Williams also serves as a Military Science Instructor with the Virginia Tech Reserve Officer Training Corps.

FAQ

Common questions.

Still have a question? Email moonshotrodeo@arrowheadresearch.org.

Is Moonshot Rodeo a competition?

Moonshot Rodeo is a showcase, not a competition. There are no winners or rankings; it’s a chance to network with builders, mentors, and experts and get direct feedback on your work.

When and where is Moonshot Rodeo 2027?

Presentation day is February 4, 2027 at 3625 Potomac Avenue, Alexandria, VA 22305. The optional build sprint runs January 25–February 3, 2027.

Do I need to join the build sprint?

No. The January 25–February 3, 2027 build sprint is optional and leads into presentation day on February 4. You can apply to build with access to shared tools and expert support (you bring your own materials), bring an existing project, or present a creative solution to a problem in the field. All three ways to present share the same stage and expert panel.

Do I need a physical prototype?

No. We also welcome presentations on creative solutions to real problems, like field workarounds, new processes, or improvised tools. If you solved something hard, it fits.

What kinds of projects fit?

The 2026 projects covered autonomy, software, security, tracking, and RF systems. We also welcome field workarounds, processes, tactics, and improvised tools. The common thread is solving real operational problems.

Who presents at Moonshot Rodeo?

Military and intelligence community builders present their technical work. In 2026, presenters included active-duty soldiers and sailors, Army engineers and technicians, and a Virginia Tech Army ROTC team. Send us a note if you’re interested in presenting or unsure whether your work fits.

Can I help without presenting?

Yes. Email us to volunteer.

Have a project or field solution you want to present?

Presentation day is February 4, 2027. The application starts with a short description of your project.
